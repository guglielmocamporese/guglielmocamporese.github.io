Emotion Geometry in LLMs:
Finding and Moving the Feeling

Guglielmo Camporese · May 2026 · Zürich
TL;DR — What does it mean for a language model to “feel” happy or sad? Not in the human sense — but geometrically, inside its activations. Anthropic showed that Claude has an internal emotion map. I looked for the same structure in Qwen3-8B, then asked whether the map is merely readable or actually movable. The answer: both. Valence and arousal form a near-orthogonal emotion plane; steering along the valence direction shifts the tone of ambiguous generations; and the causal effect is sharply localized, with positive affect concentrated around layers 12–16 while negative affect is spread across a wider window. The emotion map is not just there. It has a handle.

Background

In 2024, Anthropic showed that Claude has internal representations of emotional states arranged in a geometrically structured space [1]. The model was not explicitly trained to represent emotions this way; the structure appears to emerge as a byproduct of predicting text written by humans. The finding was important, but it left three questions open:

  1. Generalisation: does this hold beyond Claude, or is it an artifact of that specific model family?
  2. Causality: Anthropic showed the geometry exists and correlates with behaviour. But does it causally shape what the model generates? Can you move it?
  3. Where: if the geometry is causal, where in the network does the influence live? Is any layer equivalent, or is there a specific window?

This post studies those questions on Qwen3-8B — a fully open model that Anthropic's paper did not study.

Setup

Stimuli. 120 prompts across a 2×4 grid: valence (positive/negative) × arousal (high/low), plus 24 neutral controls. Three types: first-person statements ("I just got the promotion"), narrative snippets, and factual setups. The mixture tests whether the geometry is semantic or purely syntactic.

Model. Qwen3-8B-Instruct via HuggingFace Transformers [3]. 36 layers. Activations extracted at the last token position via register_forward_hook on each transformer block.

Probes. Logistic regression, 5-fold CV, one probe per layer. Binary: positive vs negative valence, high vs low arousal.

Directions. Mean-difference vectors: valence_dir = mean(pos_acts) − mean(neg_acts) at the critical layer, normalised to unit length.

Steering. A register_forward_pre_hook adds α × valence_dir to the input hidden states before the target layer runs, so the modified representation propagates through all remaining layers. Prompts use the model's native chat template via tokenizer.apply_chat_template. Sentiment measured with cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest — a fine-tuned RoBERTa sentiment model that handles negation and implicit sentiment better than lexicon matching.

Reproducibility details. Model checkpoint: Qwen/Qwen3-8B-Instruct. Activations are taken at the final prompt token before generation. Probes use logistic regression with 5-fold cross-validation. Steering uses a unit-normalised mean-difference direction. Unless otherwise stated, generations use the same decoding settings across all α values. Full prompts, seeds, generation parameters, cached activations, and plotting code are available in the linked repository.

Finding 1 — The geometry exists, and matches psychology

Both probes reach ~97% accuracy and plateau from around layer 12 onward.

One thing worth noting: arousal leads valence in the early layers. Arousal (energy level: calm vs activated) is already at 70% accuracy at layer 0. Valence (hedonic tone: good vs bad) starts lower, dips at layer 1, then recovers. The model appears to make intensity linearly available before hedonic tone — a sequence that resembles findings in affective neuroscience [4].

The geometry matches the psychological circumplex model [5] — two near-orthogonal axes:

The cosine similarity between valence and arousal direction vectors is −0.038 — nearly perfectly orthogonal. The four quadrants align with Russell's model: excited (positive/high), content (positive/low), distressed (negative/high), depressed (negative/low). The model was not explicitly trained with this circumplex objective; the structure appears to emerge from language modeling.

Here's how the structure emerges layer by layer:

At layer 0, all 120 prompts collapse into a line. By layer 16 the neutral cluster separates as a distinct region — suggesting that emotional neutrality is represented as an active state, not merely as an absence of signal. By layer 20 the separation is clean.

This provides evidence that Anthropic's finding generalises beyond Claude. At minimum, the same kind of emotion geometry appears in a fully open model with a different training lineage.

Finding 2 — The geometry is causally active

Existence and causation are different things. Anthropic showed the geometry correlates with behaviour. The question is whether you can move it and have the output follow.

Activation steering: add α × valence_dir to the residual stream at inference [6]. I used eight ambiguous prompts with no strong emotional prior — the model has to invent the tone:

"I walked home alone through the city."
"I looked out the window for a long time."
"I sat at my desk for several hours."

Actual model outputs for "I walked home alone through the city."

−20"the streets felt like a mirror, reflecting my loneliness back at me. Every shadow was a stranger, every light a lie."
0"the silence was louder than the noise of the streets. My footsteps echoed in a way that made me feel both isolated and strangely connected."
+20"the city whispered to me, each step a quiet conversation with the shadows. I felt the weight of solitude lift."

Same prompt. Same model. Same weights. Only the internal activation at layer 19 changes.

RoBERTa sentiment runs from −0.85 at α = −40 to +0.85 at α = +40 — a clean gradient across the full range. Coherence stays near 100% throughout. This suggests that the geometry is not merely decodable from activations; at the right layer, it is causally upstream of generation.

Finding 3 — There is a sharp causal window

The most practically important finding: the injection layer matters enormously.

Positive steering peaks at layers 12–16 (+0.75), then collapses sharply. By layer 20 the effect is near zero; by layers 28–35 it has inverted to negative. Inject too early and the representation has not yet become causally useful. Inject too late and generation may already be committed.

Negative steering behaves differently — strong and sustained from layers 8 through 20, weakening only in the final third of the network.

This asymmetry is the most unexpected finding. Positive affect has a narrow causal bottleneck — layers 12–16. Negative affect is more distributed — it can be injected across a wider window and still shape output.

One interpretation is that negative emotional states — grief, anger, anxiety, dread — are more varied in training data, so the model maintains their representations across more layers. Positive affect may be more stereotyped and compressed into a narrower representational window. This is speculative, but the causal window and the positive/negative asymmetry are both natural targets for follow-up experiments.

Discussion

Why this matters for alignment

More broadly, emotion is a useful test case for mechanistic interpretability because it has three properties at once: it is psychologically structured, behaviorally visible in generations, and linearly accessible in activation space. That makes it a good domain for studying when representations become causally actionable rather than merely decodable.

The causal window finding has direct practical implications:

Monitoring: a probe running on activations at layers 12–16 could flag the emotional trajectory of a generation before it completes — potentially faster and more reliable than post-hoc output classifiers.

Attack surface: the valence direction can be injected from outside. The magnitudes required (α ≥ 10) are large relative to typical residual stream norms, but the mechanism is reliable. This is worth understanding for deployed systems.

Positive/negative asymmetry: if you want to suppress negative affect, the wider causal window means more leverage — injection anywhere from layer 8 to 20 will have an effect. Suppressing positive affect requires hitting the narrow layers 12–16 window specifically.

What I'd do next

  • Cross-family validation: run on Llama-3.1-8B. Is the causal window always around 33–50% depth, or does it vary by architecture?
  • Arousal steering: does injecting the arousal direction shift intensity without changing hedonic tone? The circumplex model predicts the axes should be independently controllable.
  • Training checkpoints: does the geometry appear suddenly or build gradually during pre-training? The Pythia suite would allow this.
  • Norm-calibrated interventions: how small can α be while preserving the same qualitative layer profile? Does the causal window survive when steering is scaled relative to residual-stream norms?
  • Process supervision: the valence direction is a label-free signal extracted from model internals. Could it re-rank outputs, filter training data, or detect unwanted affective trajectories during generation?

Limitations

The steering prompt sample is small: 8 prompts for the α sweep and 4 prompts for the layer sweep. The layer-sweep result is therefore best read as a qualitative pattern, not a precise estimate of effect size by layer. A larger prompt set, multiple random seeds, and cross-model replication would tighten the conclusion.

The sentiment metric is also a proxy. RoBERTa is better than lexicon matching, but it can still confound affect with literary style, intensity, or genre. A stronger version of this experiment would report agreement across multiple sentiment models and a small human-rating set.

Finally, the steering magnitudes are large. This makes the causal effect easy to observe, but future work should compare α directly to residual-stream norms and test whether smaller, norm-calibrated interventions produce the same qualitative layer profile.

Conclusion

Anthropic showed that Claude represents emotions geometrically. I find evidence for the same kind of structure in Qwen3-8B — a fully open model — and then test whether that structure is causally involved in generation.

First: the geometry is causally active. Injecting the valence direction at the right layer shifts output sentiment by roughly 1.6 units on ambiguous prompts, with coherence intact. This suggests the representation is not merely correlated with output sentiment; it can help determine it.

Second: the causal influence is sharply layer-dependent. Layers 12–16 are the bottleneck for positive affect — miss that window and the effect collapses. Negative affect is distributed across a wider range. This asymmetry is new and, to my knowledge, not reported in Anthropic's original work.

The emotion map is real enough to decode, structured enough to match psychology, and causal enough to steer. It also has an address in the network.
